# Network Construction Pipeline Audit

**Date:** 2026-03-24
**Scope:** `generate_visualizer.py` (topology extraction), `build_osm_branch_table.py` (branch table), embedded data in `grid_visualizer.html` and `sced_diagnostic.html`
**Focus:** Snapping logic, high-degree nodes, split point anomalies, cross-voltage contamination, edge plausibility

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The network construction pipeline in `generate_visualizer.py` converts raw OSM transmission line GeoJSON features into a simplified bus-branch graph. The audit identifies seven distinct structural problems, four of which are severe enough to distort SCED results even with compensating rating adjustments. The problems fall into two categories:

**Algorithmic defects** (bugs or design flaws in the code):
1. First-match clustering bias produces order-dependent, suboptimal junction clusters
2. Snap radius gap (500 m admission vs. 150 m snapping) orphans 6.3% of 138 kV substations
3. BFS contraction creates phantom split points (531 split points at degree 0, 1, or 2 -- impossible by design)
4. HV/MV pass separation allows duplicate edges and cross-voltage contamination

**Structural limitations** (inherent to the OSM-to-graph approach):
5. Nearly radial 138 kV topology (mesh ratio ~1.06) because OSM captures corridors not meshes
6. Missing explicit transformer branches at the 345/138 kV interface
7. Large substation complexes represented as points, not areas, causing endpoint missnap

The high-degree node question (e.g., W. A. Parish at degree 8) is largely **not** an artifact -- most high-degree 345 kV substations are genuine backbone hubs. The real problem is the opposite: the 138 kV network is **too sparse**, with 27% of substations being leaves (degree 1) and 194 nodes completely isolated.

## 2. Pipeline Architecture

The pipeline has two stages:

### Stage 1: `generate_visualizer.py` (topology extraction)

```
OSM GeoJSON (lines + substations)
  |
  v
[load_line_features] -- filter >= 138 kV, inside ERCOT (PIP)
  |
  v
[load_nodes] -- substations >= 138 kV, within 500m of line endpoint, inside ERCOT
  |            -- intra-complex dedup (200m same-kV)
  v
[process_lines] -- split features into HV (>=345 kV) and MV (138-344 kV)
  |
  v
[_graph_pass] x2 -- per-tier: cluster_endpoints -> snap to substations -> BFS contraction
  |                  HV: snap_km=0.45, cluster_r=0.15
  |                  MV: snap_km=0.15, cluster_r=0.10
  v
[post-filter] -- drop self-loops and edges < 300 m
  |
  v
grid_visualizer.html (NODES: 4,303; EDGES: 4,656)
```

### Stage 2: `build_osm_branch_table.py` (branch parameters)

```
grid_visualizer.html (NODES + EDGES)
  |
  v
[impedance + rating assignment] -- X_pu from length, MVA from voltage tier + cables lookup
  |
  v
[synthetic transformers] -- substations < 300m at different voltage tiers
  |
  v
[connected component filter] -- keep largest component only
  |
  v
[post-processing] -- SPL smart ratings, plant outlet stubs, 345 kV upgrades
  |
  v
branch.csv (~4,501 rows after component filter)
```

## 3. Detailed Analysis of Snapping/Topology Construction

### 3.1. Junction Clustering (`cluster_endpoints`, lines 409-432)

**Algorithm:** For each line feature, take both endpoints. For each endpoint, iterate linearly through existing clusters. If a cluster is within `cluster_r` km, merge the endpoint into that cluster (running centroid average). Otherwise, create a new cluster.

**Problem: First-match bias.** The algorithm breaks on the *first* cluster within `cluster_r`, not the *nearest*. This is order-dependent: if clusters A and B are both within 150m of an endpoint, whichever was created first always wins, regardless of which is closer.

```python
# generate_visualizer.py, lines 417-423
for c in clusters:
    if abs(c[0] - lat) > 0.01 or abs(c[1] - lon) > 0.01:
        continue
    if haversine(lat, lon, c[0], c[1]) <= cluster_r:
        matched = c
        break       # <-- FIRST match, not nearest
```

**Impact:** In dense urban areas where multiple clusters exist within 150m, endpoints are assigned to clusters based on processing order (which follows GeoJSON feature order, which is arbitrary). This can split co-located endpoints into different clusters or merge distant endpoints into the wrong cluster. The effect is localized -- most of Texas is sparse enough that only one cluster is within range -- but Houston and DFW are exactly the areas where this matters most.

**Severity:** Medium. The centroid-averaging partially compensates, but the ordering effect means the same input data could produce different topologies if features were reordered.

### 3.2. Snap Radius Gap (500m admission vs. 150m snapping)

**The problem:** `load_nodes` admits substations that are within **500m** of any line endpoint (`CONNECT_KM = 0.5`). But `_graph_pass` snaps junction clusters to substations within only **150m** (`snap_km=0.15` for MV). A substation that is 200-500m from its nearest line endpoint passes the admission filter but can never be reached by the snapping step.

```python
# load_nodes, line 275:
CONNECT_KM = 0.5   # admission threshold

# process_lines, line 654:
mv_edges, mv_splits = _graph_pass(
    mv_feats, nodes, node_grid,
    snap_km=0.15, ...)   # snapping threshold -- 3.3x tighter
```

**Evidence:** 160 of 2,551 138 kV substations (6.3%) are completely isolated (degree 0). The HV pass uses a wider 450m snap, which is why only 12 of 452 345 kV substations are isolated. The gap specifically affects 138 kV substations in areas where OSM line endpoints are offset from substation centroids -- common at large substations mapped as areas in OSM, where the GeoJSON point is the centroid but line endpoints touch the perimeter.

**Severity:** High. 160 orphaned substations means 160 buses that exist in bus.csv but have no branches. Any load assigned to them is automatically shed. After the connected-component filter in `build_osm_branch_table.py`, these are dropped -- but the filter runs on the extracted topology, so their load disappears entirely.

### 3.3. BFS Contraction and Phantom Split Points

**Design intent:** The `_graph_pass` function performs BFS from "stop" junctions (substations + degree-3+ non-substation junctions, called "split points"). The BFS contracts chains of degree-2 junctions into single edges. Split points are supposed to have degree >= 3 by construction.

**The problem:** The final topology contains:
- 363 split points at degree 2 (pass-through nodes that should have been contracted)
- 150 split points at degree 1 (dangling stubs)
- 18 split points at degree 0 (completely orphaned)

These 531 anomalous split points (42% of all 1,266 split points) should not exist. They arise from several causes:

1. **Cross-pass contamination.** HV and MV passes run independently. A junction that is degree-3 in the MV pass might connect to only 2 MV substations (the third neighbor was an HV substation handled in the HV pass). The split point is created but ends up with degree 2 in the final merged graph.

2. **Edge dropping.** The post-filter (step 4) drops edges shorter than 300m. If a split point had exactly 3 edges and one was < 300m, it becomes degree 2 after filtering.

3. **The 300m floor vs. clustering radius interaction.** MV junction clusters are formed at 100m radius, but edges shorter than 300m are dropped. A split point formed between two clusters at 200m separation will have its edges deleted.

**Severity:** High. Each degree-2 split point adds an unnecessary node to the PTDF matrix (increasing computational cost) and creates two short branches where one would suffice. Each degree-1 split point is a dead-end stub that traps any power flowing into it. The 18 degree-0 split points are harmless (filtered by the connected-component step) but indicate pipeline logic errors.

### 3.4. HV/MV Pass Separation and Duplicate Edges

The HV (>=345 kV) and MV (138-344 kV) passes run independently, each with their own junction clusters, snap radii, and BFS contraction. Edges from both passes are then merged.

**Problem 1: Duplicate edges.** When a 345 kV line and a 138 kV line run between the same two substations, each pass creates an edge. The final graph has 9 duplicate edge pairs, all connecting the same node pair at different voltage levels:

- Temple Switching Station <-> Temple Pecan Creek: kv=[345, 138]
- Hebron Substation <-> Northwest Carrollton: kv=[345, 138]
- Cedar Bayou Plant <-> OSM_51445934: kv=[345, 138]
- etc.

The `build_osm_branch_table.py` deduplicates by `(min(a,b), max(a,b))` pair, keeping only the first edge seen. This means 9 parallel circuits are silently dropped.

**Problem 2: Cross-voltage edge classification.** 11 edges in the HV pass (classified as >= 345 kV) connect two substations that are both tagged as 138 kV in OSM. This happens because the HV pass uses the OSM line's voltage tag (which may be wrong) rather than the endpoint substation voltages. Example: Boerne Cico <-> Talley Road, both 138 kV substations, connected by an edge classified as 345 kV.

**Problem 3: 68 HV edges terminate at 138 kV substations** (non-split). This occurs because the HV snap radius (450m) is wide enough to grab 138 kV substations that happen to be near a 345 kV line endpoint. The resulting edge is classified as 345 kV but connects to a 138 kV bus, which `build_osm_branch_table.py` then rates at 1200-2400 MVA -- far too high for 138 kV equipment.

**Severity:** Medium. The duplicates lose 9 parallel circuits. The cross-voltage classification gives incorrect impedances and ratings to ~80 branches.

## 4. Specific Problematic Patterns

### 4.1. High-Degree Nodes Are Mostly Real

Contrary to the initial suspicion, the highest-degree nodes are genuine 345 kV backbone substations:

| Node | Degree | kV | Analysis |
|------|--------|-----|---------|
| Royse Substation | 12 | 345 | 5 HV edges + 7 MV edges. But the 119 km edge to Shamburger is suspicious -- likely BFS contraction skipped intermediate substations |
| NW Carrollton | 10 | 345 | 4 HV + 6 MV edges. Has duplicate edge to Hebron (345 + 138 kV) |
| Lon Hill | 10 | 345 | Genuine South Texas hub |
| P.H. Robinson | 10 | 345 | Houston switching station, known calibration problem |
| W.A. Parish (345 kV) | 8 | 345 | 3 HV + 5 MV edges. Connections are geographically plausible (0.7-78 km) |

The 345 kV backbone having degree-8-12 hubs is physically reasonable -- these are major switching stations with many incoming/outgoing lines. The W.A. Parish degree-8 result is **not** an artifact; it reflects the real station's role as a major generation hub southwest of Houston.

### 4.2. The Real Problem: Excessive Leaf Nodes

- **823 substations (27.1%) are degree-1 leaves.** In a real transmission network, leaf substations are uncommon (they represent dead-end loads or generation plants). A 27% leaf ratio indicates that many junctions where lines should connect are not being formed.

- **150 split points are degree-1.** By construction, split points are T-junctions (degree >= 3). A degree-1 split point means two of its three edges were dropped or its neighbors were snapped to different clusters.

### 4.3. Long-Distance Edges (BFS Over-Contraction)

The BFS contraction can create very long edges when intermediate junctions fail to snap to any substation and are not degree >= 3 (so they don't become split points):

| Edge | Length | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| Horse Hollow Gen Tie <-> Horse Hollow Kendall Tie | 267 km | Plausible for a long gen-tie line |
| TUCO <-> Oklaunion North | 247 km | BFS skipped every intermediate junction |
| Tonkawa <-> SPL_3090 | 205 km | Should have intermediate substations |
| Royse <-> Shamburger | 119 km | Clearly missing intermediate stops |

15 edges exceed 100 km. 133 edges in the 138 kV tier exceed 30 km. While some long edges are genuine (long rural lines in West Texas), many represent BFS contraction that failed to stop at intermediate substations.

### 4.4. Cross-Voltage Contamination at W.A. Parish

W.A. Parish illustrates the cross-voltage problem concretely:

```
W.A. Parish Station (345 kV), i=1937:
  0.66 km -> SPL_2783 (kv=138, split=False) edge_kv=345
  0.74 km -> SPL_3029 (kv=345, split=False) edge_kv=345
 14.42 km -> SPL_2317 (kv=345, split=False) edge_kv=345
 22.93 km -> O'Brien Substation (kv=138, split=False) edge_kv=345
 27.70 km -> Jeanetta Substation (kv=345) edge_kv=345
 30.65 km -> Oasis Substation (kv=345) edge_kv=345
 31.90 km -> SPL_3057 (kv=345, split=True) edge_kv=345
 77.84 km -> Hillje Substation (kv=345) edge_kv=345
```

SPL_2783 (i=2783) is tagged as 138 kV but connected via a 345 kV edge. This creates a branch that `build_osm_branch_table.py` will rate at 345 kV levels (1200-2400 MVA) even though the physical equipment is 138 kV. Similarly, O'Brien Substation (138 kV) is connected by a 345 kV edge at 23 km distance.

Note that SPL_2783 has `split=False` -- it is classified as a substation, not a split point, despite having an SPL-style index. This is because the node was originally loaded as a substation from OSM but happens to have an index >= 2037 (the split point index range). The naming is misleading but not a bug.

## 6. Root Cause Analysis

### Why are there so many leaf nodes (degree 1)?

Three interacting causes:

1. **Snap radius too tight for MV (150m).** OSM substation points are centroids of the mapped area. Large substations (especially in urban areas) have line endpoints 200-500m from the centroid. The 150m MV snap misses these, so the line endpoint becomes an unsnapped junction. If it has degree < 3, the BFS contracts it into a longer edge to/from a more distant stop -- or it becomes a dead-end stub.

2. **Intra-complex deduplication (200m same-kV).** Two OSM features at the same substation complex might be 150-200m apart. One gets kept, the other is deduped. If a line endpoint is within snap range of the removed duplicate but not the surviving representative, that endpoint becomes unsnapped.

3. **300m edge filter.** After BFS contraction, edges shorter than 300m are dropped. If a substation's only connection is via a short edge (common at bus sections within a switchyard), it becomes a leaf or isolated node.

### Why are 345 kV nodes grabbing 138 kV connections?

The HV pass uses a 450m snap radius and considers ALL features >= 345 kV. When a 345 kV substation is near a 138 kV line endpoint (common at transformer substations), the 450m snap grabs the 138 kV endpoint. The resulting edge is tagged as 345 kV (the maximum of the edge kV and node kV), giving incorrect impedance and rating.

### Why are there 204 km edges?

The BFS contraction walks from one stop junction to the next. If a line has 10 intermediate junctions but none of them are within snap range of a substation AND none have degree >= 3, the BFS contracts the entire chain into a single edge from the first stop to the last. In West Texas, this can span hundreds of kilometers.

## 7. Recommended Fixes (Prioritized by Impact)

### Priority 1: Increase MV snap radius to 300-500m (HIGH IMPACT)

**Current:** `snap_km=0.15` for MV features.
**Proposed:** `snap_km=0.40` (matching HV behavior more closely).

**Rationale:** This is the single change that would recover the most isolated substations and dead-end stubs. The 500m admission threshold already guarantees substations are near lines; the 150m snap is simply too tight.

**Risk:** Wider snap could cause distant endpoints to snap to wrong substations in dense areas. Mitigate by using nearest-match instead of first-match.

**Code location:** `generate_visualizer.py`, line 654, `snap_km=0.15`.

### Priority 2: Replace first-match clustering with nearest-match (MEDIUM IMPACT)

**Current:** `cluster_endpoints()` breaks on first cluster within `cluster_r`.
**Proposed:** Find ALL clusters within `cluster_r`, pick the nearest one.

```python
# Current (line 417-423):
for c in clusters:
    if haversine(lat, lon, c[0], c[1]) <= cluster_r:
        matched = c
        break

# Proposed:
best_c, best_d = None, float('inf')
for c in clusters:
    if abs(c[0] - lat) > 0.01 or abs(c[1] - lon) > 0.01:
        continue
    d = haversine(lat, lon, c[0], c[1])
    if d <= cluster_r and d < best_d:
        best_c, best_d = c, d
matched = best_c
```

**Code location:** `generate_visualizer.py`, function `cluster_endpoints`, lines 409-432.

### Priority 3: Handle duplicate edges from HV/MV pass merge (MEDIUM IMPACT)

**Current:** `build_osm_branch_table.py` deduplicates by `(min(a,b), max(a,b))` pair, keeping the first edge. 9 parallel circuits are silently dropped.
**Proposed:** When duplicate edges exist at different voltage levels, keep both as parallel circuits (lower the pair key to include voltage tier), or sum their ratings.

**Code location:** `build_osm_branch_table.py`, lines 237-241.

### Priority 4: Clean up phantom split points (MEDIUM IMPACT)

After merging HV and MV edges, re-check split point degrees. Degree-2 split points should be contracted (merge their two edges into one). Degree-1 and degree-0 split points should be removed.

This is a post-processing step that could be added after line 662 in `generate_visualizer.py`.

### Priority 5: Add cross-voltage validation (LOW IMPACT)

After edge construction, flag edges where the edge kV tier is >= 2 tiers above both endpoint node kV values. These are likely misclassified. Either reclassify the edge to the lower voltage or drop it.

**Evidence:** 11 edges classified as 345 kV connect two 138 kV substations. 68 HV edge endpoints terminate at 138 kV substations.

### Priority 6: Replace 300m edge filter with smarter logic (LOW IMPACT)

The blanket 300m minimum edge length drops legitimate bus-section connections. Replace with: keep edges < 300m only if both endpoints are at the same substation complex (e.g., same OSM name or within 500m and same voltage).

### Priority 7: Add explicit transformer branches (ARCHITECTURAL)

The model currently has no explicit 345/138 kV transformer branches. The 32 synthetic transformers (within 300m, different voltage) are too few. Real ERCOT has 500-1500 MVA transformers at every 345 kV substation. This is the root cause of the "radial 138 kV island" problem identified in session_2026-03-24: power cannot flow from the 345 kV backbone into the 138 kV distribution network at realistic ratings without explicit transformer modeling.

**Approach:** For every 345 kV substation, find all 138 kV substations within 2-5 km. Create transformer branches with X=0.12 pu and rating = 500-1000 MVA. This is a larger change but would eliminate the need for the 999k SPL workaround.

## 8. The W.A. Parish Question -- Resolved

W.A. Parish Station (345 kV) has degree 8. This is **not** an artifact. The station is a major generation hub (3,653 MW total capacity) with connections to:
- 3 nearby split/junction points (< 15 km)
- O'Brien Substation (23 km, 138 kV -- cross-voltage issue)
- Jeanetta Substation (28 km, 345 kV)
- Oasis Substation (31 km, 345 kV) -- this is the Meadow-Oasis corridor
- Hillje Substation (78 km, 345 kV)

All connections are geographically plausible. The cross-voltage issue with O'Brien and SPL_2783 (both 138 kV) is a minor concern (Priority 5 fix) but does not explain the degree. The real ERCOT W.A. Parish switching station likely has a similar number of line terminations.

## 9. Summary of Findings

| Finding | Severity | Nodes Affected | Fix Priority |
|---------|----------|---------------|-------------|
| MV snap radius gap (150m vs 500m) | High | ~160 isolated subs | 1 |
| Phantom split points (531 of 1,266) | High | 531 nodes | 4 |
| First-match clustering bias | Medium | Unknown (order-dependent) | 2 |
| Duplicate edge loss (9 pairs) | Medium | 18 nodes | 3 |
| Cross-voltage edge misclassification | Medium | ~80 edges | 5 |
| 300m blanket edge filter | Low | Unknown | 6 |
| Missing explicit transformers | Architectural | All 345/138 kV interfaces | 7 |

The single highest-impact fix is increasing the MV snap radius from 150m to 300-500m. Combined with nearest-match clustering (Priority 2), this would likely recover most of the 160 isolated 138 kV substations and significantly reduce the leaf-node fraction.

The phantom split point problem (Priority 4) is the largest in terms of node count but lower impact on SCED results because degree-2 split points are electrically transparent (they just add unnecessary intermediate buses).

The missing transformer problem (Priority 7) is the most important architectural issue but requires the most work. It is the root cause of the compensating-error regime where 999k SPL branches serve as implicit transformers.
