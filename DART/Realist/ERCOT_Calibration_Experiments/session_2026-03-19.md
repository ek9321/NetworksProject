## Key Findings

### 1. SPL Junction Artifact (no-spl-cap)
`v5` had 47 branches binding at ≥98% utilization; 32 had synthetic split-point (SPL_) endpoints.
Removing SPL caps cut shed from 6,298 → 2,180 MW (65% reduction). Zero SPL branches remained binding.
Remaining binding was 34 real-substation branches, entirely intra-zone (DFW NORTH, Houston, South Texas).
**Morgan Creek→Tonkawa (WEST 345 kV) did NOT bind** — west-east corridors are not the problem at constant load.

### 2. Load Filter Made Things Worse (no-spl+load-fix)
Restricting load to named (non-OSM_, non-_Plant) substations **increased** shed from 2,180 → 3,411 MW.
Unnamed `OSM_XXXX` nodes are legitimate distribution substations that just lack OSM name tags.
Removing them concentrates load on specific named DFW neighborhoods, creating new binding 138 kV patterns.
**Conclusion:** Don't filter load by name. OSM nodes should keep getting load.

### 3. Doubling 345 kV Cleared HV Congestion but Not Overall (no-spl+hv2x)
All 7 binding 345 kV lines cleared (zero HV congestion). But shed only dropped 7% (2,180 → 2,033 MW)
because freeing 345 kV pushed more power into the 138 kV distribution network, adding 6 new binding 138 kV lines.
**Conclusion:** 138 kV urban network is the true bottleneck, not 345 kV.

### 4. Doubling 138 kV Nearly Solved Everything (no-spl+138kv2x)
Shed dropped from 2,180 → 136 MW (94% reduction). Renewables utilization 47% → 68%.
Curtailment dropped 10,490 → 6,393 MW.
**Conclusion:** OSM cables tags systematically assign single-circuit (300 MVA) to urban 138 kV lines
that are almost certainly double-circuit (600 MVA) in reality.

### 5. Plant Outlet Fix Reduced Residual (best-so-far)
Added `NO_PLANT_CAP` env var: unconstrain branches where one endpoint has `_Plant` in name,
or haversine distance < 0.5 km (bus sections). Shed: 136 → 87 MW.

## Calibration Target Pivot: Wrong Day, Wrong Congestion

**The most important finding of this session came from the ERCOT IMM Monthly Report (November 2025).**

ERCOT's real top RT constraints by congestion rent (YTD 2025):
1. VEALMOOR-KOCHTAP — West TX, Permian Basin wind export
2. TWINBU-HARGROVE — West TX, Twin Buttes wind
3. LNGSW-CONSW — West TX, Longswitch area
4. BELCNTY-SALSW — Bell County → Salado, I-35 Central TX corridor
5. WESTEX GTC — West Texas Export nomogram (main structural limit)
6-7. ODEHV-YARBR, SCRCV-KNAPP — West/South TX wind
8. LARDVNTH-LASCRUCE — Laredo Viento, South TX wind
9. STP-WAP — South Texas Project nuclear outlet
10. RNKSW — Rankin Switch, West TX internal
**Most frequent by SCED intervals: Hamilton GTC (#1), then VEALMOOR-KOCHTAP (#2)**

**None of these match our binding branches (DFW urban 138 kV, Houston 345 kV ring).**

### What this means:
- November 5, 2025 was an unremarkable operating day (near-zero shortage adder, normal conditions).
  Calibrating against it was reasonable as a baseline but gives us no real LMP spread to target.
- Real ERCOT congestion is predominantly **West Texas wind export congestion** — the Permian Basin
  trying to push power east through a limited transmission corridor (WESTEX nomogram).
- Our model, with constant load and flat CFs, doesn't stress these corridors because WEST zone load
  is modeled proportional to population (small) while generation is large but CFs are constant.
- The urban DFW/Houston 138 kV congestion we've been fixing is a **flat-load artifact**, not a
  real congestion pattern. At realistic hourly loads, overnight/morning hours have low load and
  these bindings don't occur.

### What the right calibration day looks like:
- **High wind output in West Texas** — spring (March–May) or fall when Permian Basin wind runs 70-90% CF
- **Moderate to high system load** — enough demand pressure to stress export corridors
- **Large WEST vs NORTH LMP spread** — WEST price significantly below NORTH price for sustained hours
- **WESTEX nomogram binding** — validates that our west-east transmission corridor capacity is right
- **Offer curves available** — must be ≥60 days prior to today (March 19, 2026), i.e., before Jan 18, 2026
  → any 2025 date works for the 60-day SCED disclosure

## What the Calibration Should Actually Test

Given the IMM findings, the right calibration questions are:

1. **Can our model reproduce the WESTEX export constraint?**
   - On a high-wind day, does WEST LMP come out lower than NORTH LMP?
   - Is the price spread within reasonable range of actual?
   - Do west-east corridors (Morgan Creek→Tonkawa, etc.) actually bind?

2. **Can we reproduce the correct LMP ordering across all 4 zones?**
   - Target: correct ordinal ranking ≥3/4 zones in ≥18/24 hours (from calibrationViaVatic.md)

3. **Does Hamilton GTC have an analog in our model?**
   - Hamilton County is between Austin and Abilene, on the east edge of the WEST zone.
   - This may be a transformer or substation constraint rather than a line — worth investigating.

4. **Does Hicks→Willow Creek (our #1 binding line) correspond to any ERCOT named constraint?**
   - It's a 52 km, 345 kV NORTH zone line. No match in the top 10 ERCOT constraints.
   - This suggests our NORTH congestion is still partly an artifact.
