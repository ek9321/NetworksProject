### 4. Issues with Magnitudes (jun17-hourly)
- Load shedding: 593–1923 MW (actual: 0 MW) — too high
- Houston LMPs: $583–1291/MWh (actual: ~$20–50/MWh) — driven by Meadow→Oasis saturation
- Peak-hour LMPs: $2800/MWh (actual: $35/MWh) — driven by load shedding penalty ($10k/MWh)
- Root cause: 4 binding 345 kV lines in Houston/North artificially constraining dispatch

### 5. 345 kV Double-Circuit Fix (hv2400)
SCALE_345KV=2 on clean-build (Nov 5 load): **24.1 MW shed** (down from 81.7 MW).
This nearly eliminates shedding at baseline load. Key finding: the 5 residual binding 345 kV lines
are ERCOT backbone lines that are double-circuit in reality but tagged single-circuit in OSM.

### 6. Tension: Urban Congestion Fix vs. WESTEX Physics
- Blanket SCALE_345KV=2 fixes urban congestion (Meadow→Oasis, Wharton→Addicks, Anna, etc.)
- BUT also doubles Morgan Creek→Tonkawa (the WESTEX export corridor), potentially eliminating it
- task 11 (jun17-hv2400) tests whether WESTEX binding survives when all 345 kV is doubled
- If WESTEX disappears: need targeted approach — fix urban 345 kV but NOT Morgan Creek→Tonkawa

### 7. Task 10 Result: 345 kV default=2 Barely Helps (hv-default2) **[NEW]**
- Steady-state shed: **79.6 MW** (down from 81.7 on clean-build — only 2.1 MW improvement)
- The 345 kV default=2 change only affected ~46 lines not covered by GeoJSON matches.
- Only Hicks→WillowCreek (NORTH, 52 km) was genuinely upgraded; the 4 Houston/SOUTH binding
  lines (Meadow→Oasis, Wharton→Addicks, etc.) all have GeoJSON matches and were NOT changed.
- Conclusion: the default=2 change is inconsequential. The binding urban 345 kV lines need
  explicit targeted overrides, not a default policy change.

### 8. Task 11 Result: WESTEX Eliminated by Blanket SCALE_345KV=2 (jun17-hv2400) **[NEW]**
**Critical finding.** Morgan Creek→Tonkawa max flow = 1,293 MVA (rating now 2,400 MVA).
Never binds. Zone LMP spread completely eliminated — system price flat at $22–24/MWh all 24 hours.

Comparison:
| Metric | jun17-hourly (1200 MVA) | jun17-hv2400 (2400 MVA) |
|--------|------------------------|------------------------|
| Peak load shed | 1,923 MW | 1,101 MW |
| Min load shed | 593 MW | 291 MW |
| System price range | $5–$2778/MWh | $22–$24/MWh |
| Morgan Creek max flow | ~1200 MVA (binding) | 1,293 MVA (not binding) |
| WEST-NORTH spread | Yes (all 24 hrs) | None |

Shedding improved ~45% (1293 MVA was within 93 MVA of the old 1200 MVA rating — line is still
heavily loaded, just no longer binding). But losing the WEST-NORTH spread means the model no
longer represents ERCOT West Texas export physics.

**Decision: WESTEX must be preserved. Targeted 345 kV overrides required.**
- L117_1511 (Meadow→Oasis, HOUSTON): 1200 → 2400 MVA
- L99_1752 (Wharton→Addicks, HOUSTON): 1200 → 2400 MVA
- L1605_1612 (Morgan Creek→Tonkawa, WEST): KEEP at 1200 MVA
- Other residual binding urban 345 kV lines: investigate individually

- WEST < NORTH LMP ordering holds all 24 hours on June 17

2. **Urban 345 kV congestion**: Meadow→Oasis (HOUSTON) and Wharton→Addicks binding all/most hours
   → Inflates Houston LMPs dramatically ($850+/MWh vs actual $20-40/MWh)
3. **Summer peak load shedding**: 1,923 MW at peak (actual: 0 MW)
   → Partly due to missing renewables, partly urban congestion
4. **LMP magnitudes off by 10-80x**: Load shedding penalty ($10k/MWh) drives LMPs unrealistically
   → Actual resolution needed, not just qualitative ordering
