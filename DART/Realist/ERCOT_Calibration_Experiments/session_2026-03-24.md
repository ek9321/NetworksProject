## Root Cause Analysis: Why the SPL Fix Fails

### What's actually happening (not "OSM is missing lines")

We dug into bus_detail.csv to trace exactly where load sheds and why. The findings are
specific and structural, not hand-wavy.

### Finding 1: The 138 kV network is almost purely radial

Every zone's 138 kV network has mesh ratio ~1.06 (1.0 = pure tree). Houston has
only 25 independent cycles across 435 edges. This means power must flow along
specific radial paths — there are almost no redundant routes.

| Zone    | 138kV Edges | Loops | Mesh Ratio |
|---------|------------|-------|------------|
| HOUSTON | 435        | 25    | 1.06       |
| NORTH   | 1,160      | 68    | 1.06       |
| SOUTH   | 1,093      | 97    | 1.10       |
| WEST    | 679        | 37    | 1.06       |

### Finding 2: Houston is served by 42 disconnected 138 kV islands

The 138 kV-only graph fragments into 42 separate components. The largest has only
268 nodes. These islands connect to each other ONLY through the 345 kV backbone.

The worst case: a **756 MW load island** (Glenwood, Bertwood, Channelview, Jacintoport,
Port, Haden, Liberty, Baytown, Haney) that has **zero 345 kV injection points** on the
138 kV graph. It connects to the rest of Houston ONLY through 3 unconstrained (999k)
branches:
- Port → Taylor [999k SPL]
- OSM_3240 → Cedar Bayou Plant [999k plant outlet]
- OSM_3244 → S.R. Bertron Plant [999k plant outlet]

At 250 MVA per branch, these 3 paths provide 750 MVA for 756 MW of load — right at
the breaking point, and each path has 5+ hops of series 250 MVA branches upstream.

### Finding 3: The 999k branches are proxy transformers

The real ERCOT grid has 345→138 kV power transformers rated 500–1,500 MVA at each
substation. Our model has NO explicit transformer ratings. We have only 32 synthetic
transformer branches (all at 999k MVA) plus ~30 cross-tier transitions through
SPL/plant branches.

**The unconstrained SPL/plant branches are performing the function of power
transformers.** When we constrain them to 250 MVA, we're not "fixing a rating" —
we're throttling a transformer to a fraction of its real capacity.

### Finding 4: 115 kV is irrelevant to the shedding problem

We checked: 2,791 lines at 115 kV are excluded from the model. But they're almost
entirely in West Texas (2,757/2,791). Houston and DFW have zero 115 kV lines. The
dropped voltage tier is not the cause of urban shedding.

### Finding 5: Houston has the worst load-to-topology ratio

| Zone    | Load (MW) | 138kV Branches | MW/Branch | Substations | Load/Sub |
|---------|-----------|---------------|-----------|-------------|----------|
| HOUSTON | 11,500    | 435           | 26.4      | 290         | 39.7     |
| NORTH   | 23,000    | 1,160         | 19.8      | 903         | 25.5     |
| SOUTH   | 10,469    | 1,093         | 9.6       | 866         | 12.1     |
| WEST    | 6,996     | 679           | 10.3      | 655         | 10.7     |

Houston has 33% more load per 138 kV branch and 56% more load per substation than
NORTH. It's the most stressed zone by far.

### The real fix (future work)

The issue is not "missing OSM lines" — the GeoJSON has 2,641 138 kV features in
Houston, but only ~170 constrained branches survive the pipeline. The pipeline's
graph contraction (BFS from substations, 150m snap) collapses many parallel paths
into single edges. The combination of:

1. **Missing transformer ratings** — need explicit 345→138 kV transformers at
   500–1,000 MVA per unit, not infinite-capacity shortcuts
2. **Nearly radial 138 kV topology** — real urban grids have mesh structures;
   ours is almost a tree because OSM captures corridors, not the full mesh
3. **Load concentration** — Houston gets 43.9 MW per bus (uniform county-pop
   distribution), which overloads radial endpoints

The right path forward is: add explicit transformer branches at realistic ratings
(500–1000 MVA) at 345/138 kV substations, and accept that the 138 kV network
is an approximation. The 600 MVA + SPL-unconstrain approach is a reasonable proxy
until transformer modeling is implemented.

### For the presentation

Frame as: "We traced the shedding to specific topological bottlenecks — radial
138 kV islands in Houston served by implicit transformer paths. The model correctly
identifies that Houston's sub-transmission is the delivery constraint; the magnitude
is off because we lack explicit transformer ratings. This is the clearest next
improvement."

## Round 2: Transformer Proxy Experiments (tasks 22-30)

After the root cause analysis, we attempted three iterations of "smart" SPL ratings:

### xfmr v1 (tasks 22-24): SPL-only transformer proxy
- SPL branches near 345 kV → 1000 MVA; pure 138 kV SPL → 250 MVA
- Nov 5: **6,052 MW** shed (barely changed from 6,117)
- Reason: only targeted SPL branches; critical non-SPL 345→138 kV interface branches (like L2649_3019 at P.H. Robinson) were missed

### xfmr v2 (tasks 25-27): + T-junction aggregation
- Same as v1 but pure 138 kV SPL → 500 MVA (2× for flow aggregation at T-junctions)
- Nov 5: **4,839 MW** shed; Jan 8: 756–2,707 MW

### xfmr v3 (tasks 28-30): expanded to all 345→138 kV interface branches
- ANY branch (not just SPL) where one endpoint connects to 345 kV → 1000 MVA
- SPL T-junctions still at 500 MVA
- Build output: 535 transformer proxies + 2,124 T-junction aggregation branches
- Nov 5: **1,382 MW** shed; Jun 17: completed; Jan 8: completed
- This was the first attempt that made a large improvement (6,117 → 1,382)

### Why transformer proxies aren't enough
The bottleneck isn't just at the 345→138 kV interface. The 138 kV network is
radial — branches near the root carry cumulative load from the entire subtree.
A transformer proxy fixes hop 1 but not hops 5, 6, 7 in the chain.

## CRITICAL DISCOVERY: The OSM Topology Itself Is Broken

### The pivot

After all the rating experiments, we built a diagnostic visualization
(`Realist/sced_diagnostic.html`) showing the actual SCED model network overlaid
on a dark basemap with:
- Red dots: buses shedding load (49 buses at LMP=$10,000)
- Red lines: branches at capacity (30 binding)
- Orange: stressed; green: healthy; gray: unconstrained
- Click for details on any bus/line

**When the user zoomed into the model AND the original grid_visualizer.html, the
fundamental problem became visible: the OSM-to-model topology extraction is
producing a broken network.** Lines aren't connecting properly at substations.
Segments that should form a mesh are disconnected. The "weird shit at substations"
visible in the HTML is not a rendering artifact — it's the actual topology the
SCED is solving on.

### What this means

**Every rating experiment in this project has been calibrating the wrong thing.**
The 82 MW "clean-build" result wasn't a good model with minor residual congestion —
it was a broken topology masked by 999,999 MVA unconstrained branches that
papered over disconnected segments. When we removed the 999k crutch (spl-fix),
the broken topology was exposed as 6,117 MW of shedding.

The progression tells the story:
- 600 MVA + 999k SPL = 82 MW shed (broken topology hidden by infinite ratings)
- 250 MVA + 999k SPL = 3,432 MW (still hidden, but lower rating reveals some)
- 250 MVA + 250 SPL = 6,117 MW (full broken topology exposed)
- 250 MVA + smart proxy = 1,382 MW (patching the worst bottlenecks)

### What `generate_visualizer.py` does that causes this

The pipeline: GeoJSON line features → cluster endpoints (100-150m) → snap to
substations (150m) → BFS contraction → dedup edges.

In dense urban areas (Houston, DFW), this process:
1. **Missnaps endpoints** — 150m snap radius is too tight for large substations
   where line endpoints may be 200-500m from the substation centroid
2. **Fails to form meshes** — endpoints that should connect at the same junction
   end up in different clusters, producing disconnected stubs instead of a mesh
3. **The "weird shit at substations"** visible in the HTML — line segments
   converging on a substation but not actually connecting to it or each other

### What needs to happen next

**This is a massive pivot.** The next work is NOT more rating calibration. It's
fixing `generate_visualizer.py` (or replacing it) to produce a correct topology
from OSM geometry. Specifically:

1. **Diagnose the extraction failures** — use sced_diagnostic.html to identify
   specific substations where lines aren't connecting. Trace why in the pipeline.
2. **Fix the graph construction** — likely needs: larger snap radii in urban
   areas, better endpoint-to-substation matching, handling of complex substation
   geometries (OSM often maps these as areas, not points).
3. **Validate the topology** — before running any more SCED experiments, verify
   that the extracted graph actually represents a connected, physically reasonable
   network. Node degree distributions, mesh ratio, connected components.
4. **Then re-run calibration** — with a correct topology, realistic ratings
   (250 MVA 138 kV) might actually work. The rating experiments in this session
   provide a baseline to compare against.

### For the presentation

This discovery is actually a STRONG story for the "agentic AI" framing:
- The agent systematically tested rating hypotheses (18 experiments today)
- Each failure led to deeper investigation (bus-level shedding → topology
  analysis → mesh ratio → radial tree discovery → parallel path counting)
- The investigation revealed the real problem was upstream of where we were
  looking: not ratings, but topology extraction
- The diagnostic HTML visualization made the problem immediately visible
- This is how research actually works: you calibrate the wrong knob for a
  while, then discover the right one
