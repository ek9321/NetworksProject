### Key findings

**1. Aggregate stats are good.**
All-voltage E/N of 1.46 and mean degree 2.93 *exceed* real ERCOT aggregate (E/N ~1.31, mean degree 2.61). Cross-voltage links from split-point contraction add edges. The effective substation graph is healthy.

**2. 138 kV is stronger than real ERCOT.**
E/N of 1.348 exceeds real ERCOT's 1.182 (Aksoy). Mean degree 2.70 vs real 2.36. We have *more* 138 kV connectivity than the actual grid, not less. 15.1% degree-1 is elevated but many are legitimate generation interconnection and industrial load taps.

**3. 345 kV is the weak tier but not catastrophic.**
E/N of 1.179 vs real ERCOT's 1.394 — we're at 85% of real mesh density, missing ~30 edges. 32.5% degree-1 is high (TAMU targets 13.2%), but many of these are real wind farm interconnection stubs. Real 345 kV IS the most tree-like tier — the backbone is long radial corridors, not a dense mesh.

**4. Clustering coefficient is 6x real ERCOT.**
CC of 0.195–0.206 vs real 0.032–0.062. We're creating short triangles between co-located substations. This is a snap-radius artifact from the cross-voltage merge (300m) — nearby substations that share a fenceline all get connected to each other. These false triangles carry negligible impedance and shouldn't affect SCED results.

**5. PyPSA-Eur confirms OSM extractions produce E/N ~1.25 (raw).**
Their AC-only E/N of 1.252 matches our raw (pre-contraction) 1.247 almost exactly. When they add transformers + DC links, they get 1.44 — similar to our effective 1.46. This is a characteristic of OSM extraction, not a pipeline bug.

**6. TAMU synthetic grids are deliberately over-meshed.**
TAMU 2k targets E/N of 1.54–1.66, well above real ERCOT. Synthetic grids are designed to converge in AC power flow, which requires more redundancy than a DC SCED needs. Comparing to TAMU E/N is unfair — compare to real ERCOT.

### Assessment

The topology is ready for SCED experiments. The network matches or exceeds real ERCOT on most metrics. The two gaps (345 kV spur count, elevated clustering coefficient) are understood artifacts, not errors. The 138 kV mesh is actually denser than reality.

Previous sessions attributed load shedding primarily to topology sparsity. This analysis suggests the topology is NOT the bottleneck — the problems more likely lie in branch rating assumptions, missing generation (especially 19 GW of wind), and load allocation.

## 7. SCED Experiments on V3 Topology

### 7a. Baseline run (task 46: v3r2-nov5)

V3 topology, 250 MVA 138 kV base, pop-weighted load, Nov 5 constant load.

**Result: 1,195 MW steady-state shedding.** Down from 8,622 MW (v1) and 4,306 MW (v2) at same rating. V3 topology improvement is real — bridge ratio dropped from 36% (v1) to 24% (v3) at 138 kV.

All shedding in NORTH zone (DFW metro). 29 buses shedding with LMPs $10,000–$24,000. Adjacent buses at -$1,400 to -$12,000 (negative — generation trapped behind binding lines).

### 7b. Root cause: load allocation concentrates 94% of NORTH in DFW

Population-weighted load allocation puts 21,150 MW into the DFW metro (94% of NORTH's 22,400 MW). Dallas County (2.6M) + Tarrant (2.1M) + Collin (1.1M) + Denton (1.0M) dominate the population weights. The 138 kV internal DFW mesh can't distribute 21 GW through 250 MVA single-circuit lines.

Houston also gets 93% concentration (10,716 MW) but doesn't congest because it has half the total load.

### 7c. Load allocation experiments (tasks 47-49)

Tested 3 alternatives against pop baseline, all on v3 topology at 250 MVA:

```
Method           Shed (MW)  Curtail (MW)  W-N spread   NORTH "normal" %
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
pop (baseline)    1,195        3,315        -$7.7          26%
cap75               895        2,185        +$0.4          —
uniform             695        4,678       -$18.5          —
sqrt                361        1,756       -$52.6          —
```

**Conclusion: pop is the correct allocation.** It produces the most realistic WEST-NORTH LMP spread (-$7.7). Sqrt has lowest shedding but creates unrealistic $52 W-N spread and shifts shedding to Houston. Cap75 kills the price signal entirely (W-N = +$0.4). Uniform overloads rural Houston.

The DFW shedding is a real 138 kV rating problem, not a load allocation artifact.

### 7d. OSM cables tag analysis

Checked whether binding DFW lines are tagged in OSM: **all 28 binding lines have explicit `cables=3` tags** (single-circuit, 3 conductors). OSM coverage: 88% of 138 kV lines have cables tags.

138 kV cables distribution from OSM:
- cables=3 (1 circuit): 17,889 lines (89%)
- cables=6 (2 circuits): 2,182 lines (11%)
- cables=9 (3 circuits): 45 lines
- cables=12 (4 circuits): 17 lines

The 250 MVA rating is correct per OSM data. Congestion comes from aggregate flow through many single-circuit lines exceeding their individual capacity.

## 11. Rating Approach Analysis

Read `rating_approach_report.md` which proposes a floor-based rating correction:
1. Run SCED with all ratings at 999,999 MVA (BRANCH_SCALE=0) to get unconstrained Kirchhoff flows
2. For every line where flow > 250 MVA, set rating = min(flow × 1.1, cap)
3. Run corrected SCED — one-shot, physics-based, no iteration

### Floor run results (task 54: v3-jun17-floor)
- 1,730 lines where unconstrained flow > 250 MVA (33% of all branches)
- Max flow: 4,700 MVA on a single 138 kV branch (physically impossible — missing parallel paths in OSM)
- `compute_floor_ratings.py` written to generate rating CSVs at different caps

### Initial floor-rated run (task 56: cap 800)
```
                    Round 2 (41 lines)    Floor cap 800    Blanket 600
Total shed          19,228 MW·h           5,198 MW·h       3,900 MW·h
Peak shed           2,012 MW              589 MW           1,309 MW
Houston median      $531                  $215             $72
Shed buses          51                    17               229
W<N                 24/24                 20/24            21/24
```

Floor-rated cuts shedding 73% and concentrates remaining issues in 17 buses. But Houston remains hot ($215 median). The 800 MVA cap is too conservative for corridors with missing parallel paths.

### FLOOR_RATINGS_CSV env var added to run_sced.py
Reads a CSV of (UID, MinRating) and raises each line's rating to max(current, MinRating). Generated by `compute_floor_ratings.py` from floor run's line_detail.csv.
