### Key insight: smooth tradeoff

Floor ratings trade congestion for shed along a smooth curve. Lower caps preserve more network constraints → better WEST < NORTH ordering but more load shedding. Uncapped floors eliminate ALL congestion, producing flat system-wide LMPs and zero price differentiation.

**pop^0.8 load allocation makes things worse**, not better. It shifts more load into Houston, creating additional congestion there without helping wind export from WEST. Standard population-proportional allocation is correct for zone ordering.

### Shedding geography in f1200 (best floor-only result)

91% of shed is in Houston zone (1,847 of 2,026 MW). Three buses account for 84%:
- **Blodgett_Substation** (HOUSTON): 905 MW — 45% of all shed
- **OSM_4090** (HOUSTON): 625 MW — 31%
- **Grant_Substation** (HOUSTON): 170 MW — 8%

These are Houston-area load buses fed by single-circuit 138 kV lines rated at 250–450 MVA. In real ERCOT, Houston has massive parallel 138 kV infrastructure (many double-circuit lines). Our OSM topology captures the routes but rates them at single-circuit values.

## 4. Morning Ramp Scarcity Diagnosis

### Thermal commitment analysis (t135f, hours 5–7)

| Hour | Units On | Units Off | Committed MW | Headroom MW | Demand MW | Shed MW |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 116 | 357 | 27,551 | 1,507 | 50,937 | 0 |
| 5 | 116 | 357 | 27,087 | 969 | 51,672 | 101 |
| 6 | 116 | 357 | 27,031 | 868 | 52,599 | 874 |
| 7 | 116 | 357 | 27,575 | 864 | 54,127 | 1,542 |
| 8 | 116 | 357 | 27,151 | 3,526 | 56,929 | 0 |

All 474 thermal units start ON (UnitOnT0State = 1000). The RUC immediately decommits 357 of them because overnight wind (26 GW) + committed thermal (27 GW) covers the 51 GW demand. But during hours 5–7:
- Wind is flat (~26 GW), solar hasn't started
- Demand rises by 3.2 GW (50.9 → 54.1)
- Thermal headroom is only 868 MW
- System price hits $9,000+/MWh (load shed penalty) in all zones uniformly

### Root cause: reserve_factor = 0.05

The `reserve_factor=0.05` requires 5% × demand as spinning reserves:
- Hour 6: 52,599 × 0.05 = 2,630 MW reserves needed
- Actually available: 868 MW
- Reserve shortfall: 1,367 MW

But `reserve_shortfall_penalty=1e3` is 10× cheaper than `load_shed_penalty=1e4`. So the RUC tolerates reserve shortfall rather than committing extra thermal units. When reserves hit zero, load shedding begins.

### Storage investigation

Discovered that storage.csv (289 units, 17,458 MW, 54,320 MWh) was already present in sced_inputs_v3/SourceData/ on adroit. The monkey-patch injection in run_sced.py was loading and injecting all 6 zone-aggregated storage units into the model. Added diagnostic logging to confirm: `[Storage injection] 6 units injected into model, 0 skipped`.

However, the storage-only experiment (task 69) produced **identical results** to runs without storage — 1,417 MW shed, same hours. This confirms the Egret/Vatic solver is not dispatching the injected storage elements. The monkey-patch injects them into the model dict, but the solver does not use them. **This is a known limitation** — the storage integration needs deeper investigation into Egret's storage handling.

## 5. Reserve Factor Fix

### Code change

Made `reserve_factor` and `reserve_shortfall_penalty` configurable via environment variables in `run_sced.py`:

```python
# Before:
reserve_factor=0.05,
# After:
reserve_factor=float(os.environ.get("RESERVE_FACTOR", "0.05")),
reserve_shortfall_penalty=float(os.environ.get("RESERVE_PENALTY", "1e3")),
```

Added `RFACT` parameter support and `T135` reusable variable to `run_sced_array.slurm`.

## 7. Known Issues / Next Steps

1. **Storage dispatch non-functional.** The monkey-patch injects storage elements into the Egret model dict, but they are not dispatched by the solver. Needs investigation into Egret's `StorageData` format requirements — likely missing fields or wrong field types vs what Egret expects. Not blocking (reserve factor handles the morning ramp) but needed for realistic battery modeling.

2. **Targeted upgrade set is day-specific.** The 135 lines were identified from Jun 17 binding analysis. The Nov 5/Jan 8 runs don't regress because those days have lower demand. A universal upgrade set should be the union of binding lines across all calibration days — but this matters less now that f1200 + reserve_factor provide generalized headroom.

3. **Floor ratings philosophical concern.** The f1200 floor modifies ~2,800 branch ratings based on unconstrained Kirchhoff flows. This is principled (ratings proportional to expected loading) but it's a blanket adjustment. A more targeted approach would be to identify double-circuit lines from OSM `cables` tags and upgrade only those. This preserves physically meaningful constraints.

4. **Higher peak stress test.** Jun 17 2024 peak was 74.4 GW. ERCOT has hit 80+ GW. Should test at 85–90 GW to verify headroom. Could scale hourly_load by 1.1–1.2× and re-run.

5. **Multi-day statistical validation.** Currently validated on 3 days. Should run 10–20 representative days spanning all seasons and check that WEST < NORTH ordering holds statistically. Compare WEST-NORTH LMP spread distribution against ERCOT RTM SPP data.
