# Session 2026-03-28 — Multi-Day Validation & Stress Testing

## Goal

The winning config (v3-j17-t135f-r15: 135 targeted upgrades + f1200 floor + reserve_factor=0.15) produces 0 MW shed with correct WEST < NORTH ordering on all 3 calibration days. But those 3 days were used to *build* the config. The question: **does it generalize to unseen days?**

This session runs the winning config on 6 new days spanning different regimes, plus a true stress test at 85 GW (ERCOT's 2024 record peak).

### What we're checking

For each day, the winning config must:
1. **0 MW shed** (or close — some tolerance on extreme days)
2. **WEST < NORTH zone LMP ordering** for majority of hours (wind-dominated hours)
3. **No pathological LMPs** (no $9,000 scarcity except possibly morning ramp)
4. **Reasonable price levels** ($5–60/MWh in normal hours)

Failure modes to watch for:
- **Aug 20 (85 GW)**: Could exceed generation capacity. If we see shed, is it congestion or capacity?
- **Sep 29 (low wind)**: Without wind, WEST should NOT be cheaper than NORTH. Zone ordering may flip — that's *correct physics*, not a failure.
- **Mar 29 (high wind, low load)**: Massive curtailment expected. WEST should be very cheap/negative.

### Controls

- The ONLY variable across runs is the date (and its associated hourly load/CF profiles)
- This is a pure out-of-sample test

At 77 GW, new bottlenecks emerge that weren't binding at 74 GW — additional Houston-area 138 kV lines and the SOUTH→HOUSTON 345 kV interface. This is expected behavior: targeted upgrades are day-specific. A more universal approach would be to run the binding-line iteration on Aug 20 as well and take the union.

**Sep 29 evening spike (240 MW).** The evening ramp (solar off, wind at CF 0.004) with 55 GW demand briefly exceeds committed thermal. This is the same unit-commitment mechanism as Jun 17's morning ramp. reserve_factor=0.15 isn't enough for the most extreme no-wind evening ramp.

## 4. Root Cause: Uniform Load Allocation on Spur Lines

### Corrected binding analysis

My initial report of "120 binding lines" was wrong — it used base ratings, not effective ratings after floor+upgrade. Corrected:
- **18 actually binding lines** (15 at physical ratings, 3 slightly inflated)
- **54 stressed >80%** (42 at physical ratings)

The 18 binding lines are:
- 4 links in the W.A. Parish → Newgulf chain (1 corridor, 2,400 MVA SOUTH→HOUSTON)
- 1 NORTH internal 345 kV (2,400 MVA)
- 13 last-mile 138 kV lines (250–1,000 MVA) feeding HOUSTON and NORTH load buses

### The smoking gun: Houston load distribution

Houston at hour 17 (22,721 MW total, 330 load buses):
- **122 buses × 162 MW = 19,764 MW (87% of zone load)** — perfectly uniform
- 208 buses × ~14 MW = 2,957 MW

All 122 "heavy" buses get identical 162 MW because `_nearest_county_pop()` maps them all to Harris County (pop 4.7M). Of those 122:
- Degree 1 (leaf): 27 buses (22%) → **4,374 MW behind single feeders**
- Degree ≤2 (spur): 88 buses (72%) → **14,256 MW behind spur lines**
- Degree ≥3 (meshed): 34 buses (28%)

In reality, leaf-node substations serve far less load than well-connected mesh nodes. A spur-line tap might serve 10–30 MW while a major meshed substation serves 200–500 MW. Our model inverts this — every bus gets equal load regardless of network position.

### Plan: Tract-population × Degree-weighted allocation

Full plan in `Realist/plans/degree_weighted_load_allocation.md`.

**Two-factor fix:**
1. **Census tract population** (6,896 Texas tracts, ~800 in Harris County alone) replaces county-level data. Tracts range from 1,500–12,000 people within Harris County — 8× variation vs current 1× (all buses see 4.7M).
2. **Bus degree factor** — weight ∝ degree^alpha. Leaf buses (degree 1) get proportionally less load; meshed hubs (degree 4+) get more. Configurable via `DEGREE_ALPHA` env var.

Combined: `weight = tract_pop × degree^alpha`

Data sources (both public, no API key): Census 2020 PL file (population) + TIGER shapefile (centroids). Already tested — both work.

### Why it failed

The tract+degree allocation made things **much worse** across the board. Jun 17 went from 0 shed → 16k shed; Aug 20 from 6k → 20k.

**Root cause: tract population is MORE concentrated than county population, not less.**

County-level allocation gave all Houston buses 147–162 MW (uniform within Harris County). Tract-level allocation gives Houston buses 0.1–330 MW based on the population of their nearest census tract. But high-population tracts are in the urban core — which is also where the most congested corridors are.

The degree factor compounds this: high-degree buses tend to be in the denser part of the mesh (urban core), so they get even more load. The top bus (Freeman Substation) went from 147 → 330 MW.

**The problem isn't granularity of population data — it's that our network can't deliver any allocation above ~147 MW per bus to Houston.** The county-level uniform allocation accidentally matched the network's capacity to serve Houston. Making it more "realistic" (concentrated on urban core) broke the match.

### Implication

The Aug 20 congestion problem requires either:
1. **More Houston transmission capacity** — additional parallel paths or higher ratings on existing corridors
2. **Keeping the uniform-ish allocation** — it's a compensating simplification that matches our simplified topology
3. **Degree-INVERSE weighting** — put MORE load on leaf nodes (low degree) to spread demand into the network periphery where there's unused capacity. Counter-intuitive but may match how distribution networks actually work.

The "pop" allocation remains the best we have. The targeted upgrades approach (find binding lines, double them) is more productive than allocation changes.

## 7. Feedback Commitment & Comprehensive Rating Upgrades (Evening Session)

### Feedback-based commitment — confirmed shedding is congestion, not commitment

Implemented a two-pass SCED monkey-patch in `run_sced.py` (Step 5b). After each hourly SCED, if shedding > threshold, BFS finds uncommitted thermals within N hops of shedding buses, force-commits them for min_up_time hours, re-solves.

**Result:** Pass 2 shedding was consistently equal or slightly worse than pass 1 across all 9 shedding hours on Aug 20 (e.g., 359→368, 737→743, 955→961 MW). **Shedding is 100% network-congestion driven.** The RUC is already committing the right generators.

Experiments: `fb-aug20-t135` (task 91), `fb-j17-t135` (task 92), `fb-nov5-t135` (task 93). Job 3056622.

### Iterative rating upgrades — finding the real bottlenecks

**Critical discovery:** Previous binding-branch analyses used base ratings from `clean-build/branch.csv`. The actual runs apply floor ratings + T135 upgrades, changing effective ratings substantially. Had to pull the actual `branch.csv` from adroit to find the real bottlenecks.

| Round | Config | Aug 20 Shed | W<N | Key insight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | T135 (val-aug20) | 262 MW avg | 15/24 | — |
| 1 | T138 (+3 branches) | 286 MW | 14/24 | Base ratings ≠ effective ratings — no impact |
| 2 | T144 (+6 branches) | 286 MW | 14/24 | Same as T138 — wrong branches targeted |
| 3 | T183 (+45 all binding) | **0 MW** | **6/24** | Zero shed but 345kV doubling broke W<N |
| 4 | T175 (T183 − 345kV) | **26 MW** | **7/24** | 90% reduction, moderate W<N regression |
| 5 | T167 (T175 − WEST 138kV) | 41 MW | 7/24 | Removing WEST branches made things worse |

**Best result: T175** — 26 MW avg shed (was 262, 90% reduction), 161 MW peak (was 1,099, 85% reduction). Jun 17 regression: 0 shed, 19/24 W<N (was 24/24).

### Why T183 achieves zero shed but breaks W<N

T183 doubles 8 binding 345 kV corridors (2400→4800 MVA): L3530_3532, L3532_3533, L4538_4539, L2836_3530, L2415_3533, L2598_2876, L421_3195, L421_2716. These are Houston import corridors and NORTH internal links. Doubling them eliminates the congestion that creates WEST/NORTH price differentiation — power flows too freely for realistic congestion pricing.

### Why removing WEST branches (T167) didn't help W<N

The 8 WEST zone branches removed from T175→T167 were actually carrying power INTO NORTH zone (cross-zone paths like L3879_4295: WEST→NORTH, L3911_4077: WEST→NORTH). Removing them reduced power delivery to NORTH shedding areas while not improving W<N ordering.

### Shedding root cause (confirmed)

55 branches at >95% effective utilization during Aug 20 peak. Shedding concentrates at Dallas/Richardson metro buses (OSM_4090, Knight, Polk, Richardson_Woodhaven). These are transit nodes — receiving power from upstream 345 kV and distributing through saturated 138 kV feeders. The 345 kV corridors (2400 MVA) form a hard ceiling that can't be doubled without losing congestion realism.
